1. Introduction

Auvra Tech Ltd is committed to complying with all applicable laws and regulations aimed at preventing money laundering, terrorist financing, and other financial crimes.

As a platform that facilitates digital transactions, cultural asset ownership (Creator and Contribution Badges), creator payments, and community-driven economies, Auvra maintains robust Anti-Money Laundering ("AML") and Counter Financing of Terrorism ("CFT") controls to prevent the misuse of our Platform for illegal activities.

2. Purpose

The purpose of this AML/CFT Policy is to establish a clear framework that:

  • Ensures compliance with applicable AML/CFT laws and regulations
  • Detects and prevents illicit financial activities
  • Protects the integrity of Auvra's ecosystem
  • Promotes trust and transparency among users

This Policy is designed in alignment with global best practices and applicable financial regulations, including but not limited to Nigeria's Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act and the recommendations of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF).

3. Scope

This Policy applies to:

  • Users of the Auvra Platform who engage in activities that may present money laundering or terrorist financing risks (e.g., financial transactions, high-value badge sales, cross-border payments)
  • Employees, contractors, and agents of Auvra
  • Third-party service providers and partners

Users who only use low-risk features (e.g., viewing public content, using free Contribution Badges without financial activity) may not be subject to all elements of this Policy. However, Auvra reserves the right to apply appropriate measures based on risk assessment.

Any violation of this Policy may result in suspension, termination, or legal action.

4. Customer Due Diligence (CDD)

Auvra conducts Customer Due Diligence based on a risk-based assessment. CDD is triggered by certain activities, including but not limited to:

  • Creating a Creator Badge for sale
  • Receiving or sending payments above a threshold (to be defined operationally and updated as needed)
  • Subscribing to a paid community or vault plan
  • Withdrawing or transferring significant value from the Platform
  • Any activity that otherwise presents heightened risk

Standard CDD includes: Collection of full name, email address, and basic profile information.

Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) may be required for users who:

  • Engage in high-value transactions (threshold defined operationally)
  • Are located in or transact with high-risk jurisdictions (as defined by FATF or local regulators)
  • Exhibit unusual transaction patterns or are identified as politically exposed persons (PEPs)

EDD may include verification of government-issued identification, proof of address, and source of funds documentation. Auvra reserves the right to suspend or restrict access to certain features until CDD or EDD is satisfactorily completed.

Operational Thresholds:

For the purposes of this Policy, the following thresholds apply (subject to change with notice to users and regulatory authorities where required):

  • CDD trigger: Cumulative transactions or badge sales exceeding the equivalent of $1,000 USD within a rolling 30-day period.
  • EDD trigger: Cumulative transactions exceeding $10,000 USD within a rolling 90-day period, or any single transaction above $5,000 USD.
  • High-risk jurisdiction: Any country identified by the FATF as under increased monitoring or on its call for action list.

Auvra reserves the right to lower these thresholds for users exhibiting other risk indicators (e.g., unusual transaction patterns, negative media, or law enforcement inquiries). Thresholds will be reviewed at least annually and updated as necessary to comply with regulatory guidance.

5. Transaction Monitoring

Auvra continuously monitors user activity and transactions to identify unusual or suspicious behaviour. This includes:

  • Detection of large, complex, or irregular transaction patterns (e.g., rapid creation and transfer of high-value badges, circular trading)
  • Monitoring transfers involving high-risk jurisdictions
  • Identification of patterns inconsistent with a user's stated profile or cultural activity
  • Automated and manual review systems

Suspicious activities may trigger investigation, temporary restriction of accounts, or reporting obligations.

6. Suspicious Activity Reporting (SAR)

Auvra maintains procedures for identifying and reporting suspicious activities to relevant authorities where required by law. Examples of suspicious activities include:

  • Attempts to bypass KYC requirements or provide false identity documents
  • Use of the Platform for transactions with no apparent cultural or economic purpose
  • Unusual transaction patterns with no clear explanation (e.g., circular trades, self-dealing)
  • Transactions linked to sanctioned entities, regions, or known terrorist financing networks
  • Sudden change in behaviour (e.g., a long-dormant account suddenly engaging in high-volume badge sales)

Auvra reserves the right to suspend accounts and report such activities to the Nigerian Financial Intelligence Unit (NFIU) or other competent authorities. In certain circumstances, we may be prohibited by law from notifying the user of such a report or investigation. Our reporting will comply with all applicable "no-tipping off" provisions.

7. Record Keeping

Auvra maintains accurate and secure records of:

  • User identity and verification data (KYC documents, EDD findings)
  • Transaction history (badge sales, subscription payments, USDC transfers)
  • Compliance and investigation reports (SAR filings, internal reviews)

Retention periods:

  • Legal minimum: At least five (5) years from the date of transaction or account closure, as required by applicable AML/CFT laws.
  • Internal policy: Auvra may retain certain records for longer periods where necessary for legitimate business purposes (e.g., ongoing investigations, legal proceedings, or risk management), subject to data protection laws.

Records are made available to regulatory authorities when required.

8. Risk-Based Approach

Auvra adopts a risk-based approach to AML/CFT compliance by:

  • Categorising users based on risk level (low, medium, high) according to their activity, jurisdiction, and transaction volume
  • Applying enhanced controls to high-risk accounts (e.g., more frequent monitoring, additional verification)
  • Adjusting monitoring thresholds based on user behaviour and platform growth

This ensures that compliance measures are proportionate and effective, without unduly burdening low-risk users.

9. Compliance Officer

Auvra designates a Compliance Officer responsible for:

  • Overseeing AML/CFT implementation and enforcement across the Platform
  • Ensuring regulatory compliance with all applicable laws
  • Acting as the primary contact with regulatory authorities (e.g., NFIU, SEC Nigeria)
  • Conducting internal reviews, audits, and risk assessments
  • Filing Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) where required

The Compliance Officer has full authority to enforce this Policy across the organisation, including the power to freeze accounts or transactions pending investigation.

10. Training and Awareness

Auvra ensures that all relevant personnel (including customer support, product, and compliance teams) receive ongoing training on:

  • AML/CFT regulations and obligations
  • Identification of red flags and suspicious activities
  • Internal reporting procedures and escalation paths

Regular updates and refresher programmes are conducted to maintain awareness and adapt to evolving regulatory requirements.

11. Monitoring and Review

Auvra conducts continuous monitoring and periodic reviews of its AML/CFT framework to ensure effectiveness and alignment with evolving regulatory requirements. This includes:

  • Internal audits and compliance checks (at least annually)
  • External assessments where necessary (e.g., third-party audits for licensing or partnership requirements)
  • Updates to policies and procedures in response to new regulations or emerging risks
